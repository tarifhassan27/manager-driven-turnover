# Business Problem

When turnover rises, HR usually reaches for the policy levers it already
has on hand: grade bands and span of control. A well-known claim in HR
research (Gallup: "people don't leave companies, they leave managers")
says those levers miss the point — that who someone reports to matters
more to whether they stay than their grade or the size of their team.

The two readings lead to different decisions. If turnover really
clusters by manager, retention effort belongs with manager quality. If
it doesn't, the claim is being taken at face value and the effort is
aimed at the wrong place.

A raw comparison of turnover rates by manager can't settle it. A manager
who happens to have disproportionately many new hires is not comparable
to one with a long-tenured team — the standard confound in naive
manager-turnover comparisons.

This project tests the claim on a controlled synthetic dataset: 2,500
employees under 180 managers over a 5-year observation window, with
grade and team size built in alongside manager identity as candidate
drivers of exit risk.

**The question:** does turnover cluster by manager, and does that
clustering survive controlling for grade and team size?

**The decision it informs:** if the goal is reducing turnover, whether
to look at manager quality or at grade and team-size policy levers.
